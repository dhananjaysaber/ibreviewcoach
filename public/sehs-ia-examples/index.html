SEHS IA examples: how to learn from them

Examples can help you see what a finished investigation looks like. Used well, they teach you structure and standard. Used badly, they lead to copying, which breaks IB academic integrity rules.

What to look for

  • How the research question names the variables and the group.
  • How much detail is in the method, and whether you could repeat it.
  • How the graphs are labelled and what they show.
  • How the conclusion uses numbers and compares with research.
  • How specific the evaluation is.

Compare, do not copy

Read an example with the criteria beside you. Ask where each criterion is met and how you know. Then check your own draft in the same way. Never reuse wording, data or ideas from someone else's work.

A note on the new course

Examples written for the previous SEHS course were marked against five different criteria, including Personal engagement and Communication. The course first assessed in May 2026 uses four criteria: Research design, Data analysis, Conclusion and Evaluation. Use only examples marked against the new criteria, and treat any older example as a guide to the topic, not to the marking.
