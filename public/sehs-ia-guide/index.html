IB SEHS IA guide: the Sports, Exercise and Health Science internal assessment

The IA in IB Sports, Exercise and Health Science (often just called the Sports Science IA) is one individual scientific investigation. You choose a question, collect your own data, analyse it and write a report. This guide explains how it works before you start.

What the SEHS IA is

The new SEHS course was first taught in August 2024 and is first assessed in May 2026. Its IA is called the scientific investigation. The IB describes it as an open-ended task in which you gather and analyse data to answer your own research question, and you hand in the outcome as a written report.

It is the same task at Standard Level and Higher Level. Hands-on data collection is the heart of it. Databases or simulations can support your work, but they should not be your main source of data.

The key facts

  • Task: one individual written report based on your own investigation.
  • Marks: 24 in total, from four criteria worth 6 marks each.
  • Weighting: 24% of your final SEHS grade, as set out in the IB subject brief.
  • Time: about 10 hours is recommended for the investigation, though planning, data collection and writing often take longer in real life.
  • Length: the subject brief gives a limit of 3,200 words for the report. Check with your teacher how tables, graphs and the bibliography are counted.
  • Marking: your teacher marks it, then the IB externally moderates the school's marks.
  • Emphasis: half of the marks (12 of 24) are for Conclusion and Evaluation, so the thinking after the data matters most.
  • Group work: small groups may now share a similar method early on, but each student collects their own data and writes their own report.

The four criteria in one line each

  • Research design: a focused question, a justified method and a procedure someone else could repeat.
  • Data analysis: accurate recording and suitable processing of your data, with uncertainty and variation handled properly.
  • Conclusion: an answer to your research question that is supported by your analysis and compared with accepted scientific knowledge.
  • Evaluation: an honest look at the strengths, limits and possible improvements of your investigation.

Older websites describe five criteria (Personal engagement, Exploration, Analysis, Evaluation, Communication). Those belong to the previous course. If a guide mentions them, it is out of date for 2026.

What changed from the old SEHS IA

  • Personal engagement and Communication are no longer marked as a separate criterion.
  • Conclusion and Evaluation are now two separate criteria, and together they carry 50% of the marks.
  • A 3,200-word limit applies to the report.
  • Conclusion now rewards discussing the practical implications of your findings.
  • Limited collaboration is allowed, under set conditions.
  • The course has two external papers instead of three, and the old options have been removed.

How to plan it

  1. Pick a topic from the SEHS syllabus that you can actually test with the people and equipment you have.
  2. Turn it into a narrow research question with a clear independent and dependent variable.
  3. Write a method with exact timings, distances, intensities, equipment and units. Get your teacher's approval and written consent from every participant before you test anyone.
  4. Run a short pilot to find problems early.
  5. Collect the full data set, then process it and draw clear graphs.
  6. Write the conclusion and evaluation as you finish the analysis, not at the end.

Where students lose marks

Most lost marks come from a vague research question, a method that cannot be repeated, graphs without a clear message, a conclusion that goes beyond the data, and an evaluation that lists generic problems such as human error. The pages linked below deal with each of these.
