SEHS IA conclusion and evaluation: how to score higher

Together, Conclusion and Evaluation are half of the 24 marks. Many reports lose marks here because the writing is general. Specific evidence is what moves you up a band.

Writing the conclusion

  1. Answer the research question in the first sentence.
  2. Support the answer with a few selected numbers from your analysis, such as the mean difference, the direction and size of the effect, and the variation. Do not repeat your whole results table.
  3. Say how confident you can be, based on the spread of your data and any statistics.
  4. Keep your claim within the participants and conditions you tested.
  5. Compare your result with accepted science, such as peer-reviewed studies, textbooks or established theory. Say whether it agrees and why your result is plausible.
  6. Discuss the practical implications. The new syllabus asks for this, so base it on your evidence and avoid advice for everyone.

What to avoid in a conclusion

  • Claiming a cause when you only found a link.
  • Repeating the introduction.
  • Adding results that are not in your data.
  • Ignoring results that did not fit your hypothesis.

Writing the evaluation

A good evaluation judges your investigation. For each important limitation, follow the same pattern.

  1. State it precisely: for example, all participants did condition A before condition B.
  2. Explain the effect on your data: fatigue could have lowered the results for condition B.
  3. Judge its importance: compared with your other limitations, did it reduce precision, add bias, weaken validity or limit what you can conclude?
  4. Improve it: use a counterbalanced design in which half the group does each order.
  5. Explain how the improvement solves the problem: any order effect would now be spread across both conditions.

Improvements must fix the real cause. A larger sample cannot fix a miscalibrated instrument.

Specific beats generic

  • Weak: human error affected the results.
  • Stronger: starting the stopwatch by hand added a reaction time of about 0.2 s, which is large compared with the 0.1 s difference between groups. Using timing gates would reduce this.

Strengths count too

Say what worked well and why it supports your conclusion, for example standardised instructions, a pilot test or the use of repeated trials.

Future work

Finish with one or two realistic next steps. For example, a larger sample, a different age group or a longer training period.
