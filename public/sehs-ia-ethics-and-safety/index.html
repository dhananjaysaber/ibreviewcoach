SEHS IA ethics and safety: what you need before you test anyone

Any SEHS IA that tests people needs approval and consent first. These rules come from the IB examiner instructions for the sciences. Your school may add more, so check with your teacher.

Before you start

  • Your supervising teacher must approve any planned experiment on people, after discussing it with you.
  • You need written permission from each participant, with a confidential informed consent form.
  • Participants under 16 need written consent from a parent or guardian.
  • If the activity is moderate to vigorous in intensity, you need a written declaration that each participant is in good physical health, such as a PAR-Q.

What is not allowed

  • Experiments that give participants any substance, including alcohol, drugs, medicines, dietary supplements, caffeine drinks and energy drinks.
  • Experiments that involve body fluids such as sweat, blood, urine or saliva.

This means ideas such as caffeine, creatine, energy drinks or sweat testing are not allowed. Choose a variable you can change without giving participants anything, such as warm-up type, rest time, technique or music.

What to show in your report

  • That your teacher approved the plan.
  • That you collected written consent, and how you kept names confidential.
  • How you checked participants were fit to take part.
  • The safety steps you took, such as a supervised warm-up and stopping rules.

Keep participants safe

  • Avoid maximal efforts unless you have a teacher supervising and a safe protocol.
  • Use a warm-up and a cool-down.
  • Stop the test if a participant feels unwell.
  • Do not use extreme heat, dehydration or fasting.

Ethics also earn marks

The Research design criterion looks for consent, confidentiality, safety and welfare. Showing that you thought about them is part of a strong method.
