Common SEHS IA mistakes and how to fix them

These are the problems that appear again and again in SEHS IA drafts. Check your draft against each one before you submit it to your teacher.

Research design

  • Question too broad. Narrow it to one independent variable, one measure and one group.
  • No scientific context. Add a short explanation of why you expect your result.
  • Method that cannot be repeated. Add exact timings, distances, intensities, equipment and instructions.
  • Controls missing. List what you kept constant and how.
  • Safety and consent not mentioned. State your teacher's approval, the written consent you collected and your safety steps.
  • Breaking IB rules. Do not give participants any substance, such as supplements, caffeine or energy drinks, and do not use body fluids.

Data analysis

  • Raw data missing or untidy. Show clean tables with units.
  • Graphs that do not match the data. Choose the type that shows the comparison.
  • No uncertainty. State the limits of your measurement and the variation.
  • Statistics without explanation. Say what the test shows in plain words.
  • Wrong test for the design. Use paired tests for repeated measures and unpaired tests for separate groups.

Conclusion

  • Does not answer the question. Use the first sentence for the answer.
  • Overclaims. Match your wording to the strength of the evidence.
  • No comparison with science. Add one or two relevant sources or syllabus ideas.
  • No practical meaning. Say what the result means for training or health, from your evidence only.
  • Claims that are too broad. Keep them to the people and conditions you tested.

Evaluation

  • Generic limitations. Replace human error with the specific cause and its effect.
  • No improvements. Pair every limitation with a realistic fix that targets its cause.
  • No ranking of limitations. Say which matter most and why.
  • Ignoring strengths. Say what supports the reliability of the result.

Structure and writing

  • Going over the word limit with long background theory. Keep it focused.
  • Hiding key evidence in an appendix. Keep essential data and reasoning in the main report.
  • Copying text from the internet or examples. Your work must be your own and properly cited.
  • Using old criteria. Advice about Personal engagement and Communication is for the previous course.
