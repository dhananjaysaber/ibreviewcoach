IB SEHS IA guide

SEHS IA topic ideas for the IB Sports Science investigation

These ideas are starting points, not finished research questions. Choose one that you are curious about, narrow it, and check that it is safe, measurable and approved by your teacher. IB rules do not allow experiments that give participants any substance, so none of these involve supplements, caffeine, drinks or food.

Exercise physiology

  • Warm-up type and 20 m sprint time
  • Rest interval length and repeated sprint performance
  • Breathing pattern and swim or sprint time
  • Recovery method and heart rate return
  • Time of day and grip strength or jump height
  • Cadence and heart rate during steady cycling
  • Body position and recovery heart rate

Biomechanics and skill

  • Run-up length and long jump distance
  • Arm swing and vertical jump height
  • Stance width and basketball free-throw accuracy
  • Grip type and handgrip endurance
  • Footwear and agility test time
  • Joint angle and plank hold time
  • Video analysis of take-off angle and jump distance

Fitness testing and training

  • Short plyometric programme and jump height over a few weeks
  • Stretching type and sit-and-reach score
  • Interval versus continuous training and shuttle-run score
  • Core exercise programme and balance test score
  • Training frequency and push-up endurance

Psychology and performance

  • Music tempo and 6-minute run distance
  • Visualisation and free-throw or penalty accuracy
  • Audience presence and performance on a skill task
  • Concentration cues and reaction time
  • Self-talk style and endurance task performance

Health and lifestyle (observational)

  • Resting heart rate and weekly activity level
  • Self-reported sleep duration and balance test performance
  • Screen time and reaction time
  • Posture and range of movement
  • Weekly training volume and vertical jump height

Before you pick one

  • Is there a safe, ethical way to test it at school? You need your teacher's approval and written consent from each participant.
  • Can you recruit enough participants and repeat the test?
  • Do you have the equipment, such as timing gates, a stopwatch app, a heart rate monitor or a camera?
  • Is the measure objective, so that two people would record similar values?
  • Would your teacher approve the risk level and any consent forms?

Do not plan anything that gives participants a substance, uses body fluids, or involves extreme heat, dehydration or fasting. See our ethics and safety guide.
