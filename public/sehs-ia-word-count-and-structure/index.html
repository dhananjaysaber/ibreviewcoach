SEHS IA word count and structure

The IB does not require fixed headings for the scientific investigation, but a clear structure helps your teacher and the moderator find the evidence for each criterion.

Word limit

The IB SEHS subject brief gives a maximum of 3,200 words for the report. Ask your teacher how data tables, graphs, calculations, the bibliography and any appendix are counted at your school, and keep a running word count as you write.

A structure that follows the criteria

  1. Title and research question.
  2. Background and context. A short explanation of the science behind your question.
  3. Hypothesis or expectation and the reasons for it, if you use one.
  4. Variables. Independent, dependent and controlled.
  5. Method. Equipment, participants, procedure, safety and ethics.
  6. Raw data.
  7. Processed data and graphs. Calculations, uncertainty and any statistics.
  8. Conclusion.
  9. Evaluation. Strengths, limitations, improvements and further work.
  10. Bibliography.

Spending your words

  • Keep the background short and relevant. Long theory sections are a common reason for going over the limit.
  • Spend your words where the marks are. Research design, conclusion and evaluation are all written in sentences, so give them room.
  • Use tables and graphs for data, and use words to explain what they show.
  • Do not hide essential reasoning in an appendix.

Make evidence easy to find

A moderator reads many reports. Use clear headings, number your figures, and refer to them in the text. This makes it easier for the marker to see the evidence for each criterion.
