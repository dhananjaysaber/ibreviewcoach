IB SEHS IA criteria and rubric explained

The SEHS IA (first assessed in 2026) is marked out of 24 using four equally weighted criteria. Each one is worth 6 marks, so each is 25% of the IA. Conclusion and Evaluation together carry half of the marks.

Research design (6 marks)

This criterion looks at how well your investigation is set up. A strong report has a research question that is focused and placed in a scientific context, methodological choices that are explained, and a procedure with enough detail to be repeated.

The top band is about why you made each choice, not only what you did.

  • State the independent variable, the dependent variable, how each is measured, the group you tested and the context.
  • Explain how you chose participants and any inclusion criteria.
  • Explain your sample size, repetitions and testing order.
  • Say what you controlled, and how you standardised instructions.
  • Give exact equipment, units, timings and intensities, and say how accurate your equipment is.
  • Include consent, confidentiality, safety and welfare steps.

Data analysis (6 marks)

This looks at how clearly you record and process data. Raw data should be organised, with units and sensible precision, and your processing should suit the question.

  • Present raw data in clear tables with headings, consistent units and sensible decimal places.
  • Show a sample calculation, so that processed values can be traced to your raw data.
  • Use suitable statistics such as means and standard deviation, and explain them.
  • Show uncertainty and variation, including participant variation and reaction time, not only the resolution of an instrument.
  • Use graphs that fit the question, with labelled axes, and say what any error bars show.
  • Choose a statistical test that matches the design, and explain the result in relation to your research question.

Conclusion (6 marks)

This looks at whether you answer your research question using your own analysis, then compare your result with accepted scientific knowledge.

  • Answer the question directly in the first sentence.
  • Use selected numbers from your analysis as evidence: the direction and size of the effect, the variation and any statistics.
  • Keep your claims within the participants and conditions you tested.
  • Compare your result with accepted science, such as peer-reviewed studies, textbooks or established theory, and say why your result is plausible.
  • Discuss the practical implications of your findings. These should follow from your evidence and should not be general advice for everyone.

Evaluation (6 marks)

This looks at how well you judge your own investigation. It rewards specific strengths and limitations, how they affected the data, and improvements that would fix them.

  • Name each limitation precisely, not as general human error.
  • Explain how it affected your data.
  • Judge how important it is compared with your other limitations, for example whether it reduced precision, added bias or limited what you can conclude.
  • Suggest a realistic improvement that targets the actual cause, and explain how it solves the problem.

How the bands work

Each criterion is marked in three bands: 1-2, 3-4 and 5-6. Markers choose the band that fits best, then decide between the lower and upper mark. A strength in one area, such as one excellent graph, does not make up for weak processing or uncertainty. Markers do not award marks for headings. They award marks for evidence in your report, so make that evidence easy to find.

Your teacher will give you the official band descriptors. IB IA Review compares your draft to the criteria and shows where the evidence is missing, but your teacher decides your mark.
