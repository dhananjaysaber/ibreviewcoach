1 · Criteria and marks

The four SEHS IA criteria

The IB Sports Science (SEHS) IA, first assessment May 2026, is marked out of 24 by your teacher, then externally moderated by the IB. Each criterion has levels 0, 1–2 (low band), 3–4 (mid band) and 5–6 (high band), marked on a best-fit basis. Conclusion and Evaluation together carry half of the marks.

6

Research design

A focused question with context, a justified method, and a procedure someone else could repeat.

6

Data analysis

Clear, precise recording and processing, with uncertainty and graphs that fit the question.

6

Conclusion

A justified answer, compared with scientific context, with real-world implications.

6

Evaluation

Specific strengths and limitations, their effect on your data, and realistic improvements.

2 · Word count

SEHS IA word count: 3,200 words

The limit is 3,200 words for the new syllabus (first assessment: May 2026). Older guides for the previous SEHS course quote different limits, so check the date and syllabus on anything you read.

Not counted: charts and diagrams, data tables, equations, formulae and calculations, citations and references, the bibliography and headers. Put the key data, processing and reasoning in the main report, not only in an appendix.

5 · Data analysis

Data analysis for Sports Science data

  • Record clearly. One table per measure, with units in the column headings and the same decimal places as your equipment.
  • Process to fit the question. Means for repeated trials, range or standard deviation for variation, percentage change for before and after.
  • Choose the right graph. Bar charts with error bars to compare groups, line graphs for change over time, scatter plots for relationships.
  • State uncertainty. Timer reaction time, differences between people and changes in effort all matter. Say what your error bars show.

6 · Statistics and sample size

Which test, and how many participants?

My designUsual test
Two conditions, same peoplePaired t-test
Two separate groupsUnpaired t-test
Three or more separate groupsOne-way ANOVA
Two measures that may be linkedCorrelation

The IB does not set one fixed number of participants, so ask your teacher what your school expects. More participants give a more reliable mean. Very small groups make a test unreliable, and repeating a test on one person is not the same as having more participants. A pilot shows how much your measurements vary. Choose your test before you collect data and explain what the result means for your question.

7 · Ethics, consent and safety

Ethics, consent and safety for your SEHS IA

  • Teacher approval first. Your supervising teacher must approve any experiment on people.
  • Written consent. From every participant, and from a parent or guardian if they are under 16.
  • Health declaration. Needed for moderate to vigorous activity.
  • Not allowed. Giving any substance, such as caffeine, creatine, energy drinks or medicines, or using body fluids such as sweat or blood.
  • Keep people safe. Warm up, set stop rules, avoid maximal efforts without supervision.
  • Show it in your report. Ethics and safety thinking earns marks in Research design.

8 · Timeline and checklist

Plan your SEHS IA in ten stages

The IA is about 10 hours of class time, but the thinking, pilot and writing take longer. Work backwards from your school deadline.

  1. ExploreRead the criteria and shortlist two or three feasible ideas.
  2. QuestionName the variables and the group. Get it approved.
  3. PlanMethod, controls, safety and consent.
  4. PilotRun the whole procedure once and record every change.
  5. CollectComplete, labelled tables. Back up the same day.
  6. ProcessMeans, variation, graphs and any statistical test.
  7. InterpretAnswer the question with numbers. Rank limitations.
  8. DraftCover all four criteria within 3,200 words.
  9. ImproveTurn feedback into a priority list.
  10. FinishFinal checks, referencing and submit.

9 · Common mistakes

Where Sports Science IA students lose marks

  • A vague question. No variables, no group, no unit.
  • A method nobody could repeat. Missing timings, equipment or instructions.
  • Graphs without a message. No units, no title, no error bars.
  • A test that does not match the design. For example an unpaired test on repeated measures.
  • A conclusion that claims too much. Stay within the people and conditions you tested.
  • A generic evaluation. Name the limitation, how it changed the data, and a fix.

FAQ

IB Sports Science (SEHS) IA: common questions

What is the IB Sports Science (SEHS) IA?

It is the scientific investigation in IB Sports, Exercise and Health Science. You design and carry out one individual investigation, collect and analyse your own data, and write a report of up to 3,200 words. It is about 10 hours of work and is worth 24% of the final grade.

How many marks is the SEHS IA worth and what are the criteria?

The IA is marked out of 24 using four criteria worth 6 marks each: Research design, Data analysis, Conclusion and Evaluation. Conclusion and Evaluation together carry half of the marks.

What is the SEHS IA word count?

The maximum is 3,200 words. Charts and diagrams, data tables, equations, formulae and calculations, citations and references, the bibliography and headers are not counted.

Is personal engagement still marked in the SEHS IA?

No. For the new syllabus (first assessment: May 2026), Personal engagement and Communication are no longer separate criteria. Examples written for the previous course were marked against five different criteria.

Do I need consent forms for my SEHS IA?

If your investigation involves people, your supervising teacher must approve it and you need written permission from each participant. Participants under 16 need consent from a parent or guardian. You must not give participants any substance or use body fluids.

How many participants do I need?

The IB does not set one fixed number. Ask your teacher what your school expects. More participants give a more reliable mean, and very small groups make a statistical test unreliable. A pilot test shows how much your measurements vary.

Can I work in a group?

Collaboration is optional, in groups of up to three. Each student has their own research question, their own data and their own report.

Is my IA draft kept private?

Yes. Your file is stored in a private bucket, never on a public domain, and the original is deleted about an hour after upload. We never use uploaded work as an example. If you choose to save a report to your account, the original can be kept for up to 6 months so you can download it again, and you can delete it at any time.
